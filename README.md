GENESIS

An open-world, never-ending artificial life sandbox. No objective, no fitness function, no win state. I drop a small founder population into a procedurally generated world and let a genetic algorithm plus a fixed-topology neural net run them forward. Everything else, speciation, predator/prey dynamics, body-plan divergence, herd behavior, is emergent. Nobody scripted it. Every load is a fresh seed, so no two runs converge on the same outcome, and the simulation isn't designed to stop. I built this to actually watch Darwinian selection and genetic algorithms do something, not just read about them.

Design rules I've held since day one

No fitness function, ever. Selection is emergent: survive, find food, find a mate, don't get eaten. The genome is a variable-length tree, not a fixed parameter vector, so mutation can change topology (add a part, delete a part, duplicate a whole subtree), not just tweak numbers. Hard safety caps everywhere (population, genome size, tree depth) so nothing runs away and kills the tab. Seedable RNG so runs are reproducible when I need to debug them. Extinction is a valid outcome, not a bug I patch around. Every new system gets gated behind a flag so I can switch it off and fall back to known-good prior behavior if it turns out to be wrong.

What's actually in it right now

A genome is a tree of body-part nodes (mouth, gut, tail, eye, leg, fin, wing, spike, horn, gland) plus continuous body-plan genes (elongation, neck length, leg length, overall size) plus a small MLP brain (26 in, 10 hidden, 6 out) whose weights evolve right alongside the body. Reproduction is sexual only: same species, close enough to actually meet, both past maturity and above an energy threshold. Speciation is genetic-distance clustering, not authored, a lineage that drifts far enough from its species rep just becomes a new species. Metabolism scales with body size: energy storage grows close to linearly with mass, burn rate grows sub-linearly, so small animals run a small tank at a comparatively fast burn and have to eat constantly, and big animals burn more food in total but their reserve lasts way longer. The world is a procedurally generated island on an infinite ocean with real elevation, biomes, day/night, and seasons.

Version history

V1. The starting point. Generative variable-length genome (CORE/GUT/LIMB/JAW/SPIKE/EYE/NECK), no brain at all, movement and feeding were pure reactive logic (nearest food, nearest threat, nearest prey, weighted if/else, no neural net in the loop). Reproduction was asexual only, a creature above an energy threshold just split into a mutated copy of itself. Terrain was a couple of soft water blobs on an open field, never lethal, just a speed/upkeep penalty. World wrapped at the edges (toroidal, no coastline). Population capped at 1200. I already called out the complexity plateau as "the open-endedness wall" in the footer text, so I knew from day one that the finite part vocabulary was the honest limitation.

V2. Added the neural brain (fixed 20-8-6 topology, weights start near zero so behavior begins equal to the old reactive logic and evolution shapes it from there), sexual reproduction with real tree crossover plus brain-weight crossover, genetic-distance speciation with reproductive isolation, day/night and season cycles, and toxicity (GLAND part, poison payback if you eat something toxic). Kept the old asexual path as a fallback if no mate showed up within a timer. Terrain got actual biome bands (water/sand/grass/rock/snow) off a two-octave value-noise heightmap, still no elevation rendering, just movement multipliers. Every system flag-gated so I could turn brains or sex off and get v1 back.

V3. Split the LIMB part into LEG/FIN/WING, JAW into MOUTH (herb or carn mode), NECK into HORN, added a TAIL part, made GUT/TAIL/MOUTH singleton (one per body, enforced). Removed the asexual fallback entirely, mating-only from here on. Maturity and max age both roughly quadrupled. But the actual headline change is the renderer: threw out the flat top-down circles-on-a-plane view and built the whole isometric block-tile projection from scratch, iso(), tile grid, elevation as block levels, drop-face rendering for cliffs. That's the origin of the whole blocky-isometric look the project still has.

V4. Added two real inheritable genes: scale (exponential body-size dial, 0.12x to 16x, mutated multiplicatively) and life (lifespan multiplier). Before this, size was just a side effect of how many parts you had, now it's an actual open-ended evolutionary dial, which is where the tiny-darts-vs-lumbering-giants speed tradeoff comes from. Switched the world from toroidal wraparound to a bounded rectangle with inward edge-steering, no more wrapping around the map edge. Added species-centroid tracking so an unpaired fertile creature would steer toward where its own species currently is instead of wandering blind. Raised the population/plant/species caps, widened the world.

V5. Added neurological herding and pack motives: founders now spawn in seven gaussian colony clusters instead of scattering uniformly, and I added a decoupled biological time scale (bdt vs real dt) so aging, metabolism, and breeding run slower than movement, individuals live sim-days instead of seconds. The herding behavior itself was the bug: an unpaired fertile creature's steering direction became the raw summed offset to every nearby kin creature, dirx = kinX, not normalized, not divided by count, plus an unconditional kin-cohesion pull added on top for every creature. For a dense local cluster that raw sum is in the hundreds of world-units, which completely swamped hunger and food-seeking in the direction mix. That's the actual mechanism behind the huddle-and-starve collapse. Also added a parthenogenesis fallback in this version, a fertile creature isolated for 50+ seconds reproduces alone, explicitly an Allee-effect rescue for sparse founder populations.

V6. The fix-and-expand version. Rewrote steering to accumulate weighted unit vectors instead of raw ones, the actual fix for the V5 huddling bug, plus added real separation physics so bodies physically can't overlap. Threw out the old value-noise terrain and rebuilt it as multi-octave fBm plus ridged noise: procedural island via a superellipse shape mask with a warped coastline, one real mountain massif, secondary ridges for valleys and hills, inland lakes, rivers carved down to shallow depth (never deep, so they never cut a population in half), and elevation x moisture x temperature biome classification (deep/shallow/beach/marsh/plains/forest/taiga/savanna/tundra/rock/scree/snow). Added a real canSwim gate, a creature needs actual fin mass to cross into deep water, which is what turns the sea floor into its own niche instead of empty space. Crypto-random seed by default instead of the old fixed one. Renderer got an equally heavy rebuild to match.

V7. The largest single version, built incrementally:

Body-plan genes (elongation, neck, leg length) so species are visually and mechanically distinct, giraffe-form, serpentine, shark-form, stilt-legged, squat-tank silhouettes are genuine phenotype outcomes now, not just recolored circles.
Fixed a speed-vs-mass floor bug from V4's formula that flattened speed scaling above roughly mass 8, giants actually move slower now.
Reworked metabolism to scale with body size properly (storage close to linear with mass, burn rate at mass^0.55) so small and large animals have a genuinely different relationship to food.
Doubled the island footprint twice more on top of V6, pushed mountain relief from ~5 blocks to 10-100+ with real valleys and ridgelines, gave the ocean floor real bathymetry instead of one flat plane.
Unified the simulation's and renderer's elevation grids into one shared array, fixing a bug where creatures visually stood on vertical cliff faces because sim and renderer were quantizing elevation differently.
Gated all food/mate/prey perception on actual reachability (climb height, water, flight), which fixed most of the remaining starvation deaths, creatures had been pathing toward food they could see but not physically reach.
Added active foraging once hunger crosses a threshold, and a crowding metabolic penalty so huddling is selected against instead of free.
Added species-centroid rendezvous steering and age-scaled mate urgency so sparse populations on a big map can still find each other.
Iterated reproduction three times: removed V5's parthenogenesis fallback, added a much narrower proven-survivability terminal-investment clone as a true last resort, then removed that too once rendezvous/urgency steering made it unnecessary. Currently sexual-only, a fun echo of V3 doing the exact same thing to V2's asexual fallback.
Added a phylogeny visualization: species-level dendrogram by default, full individual pedigree as a toggle, camera auto-fit rewritten to scale horizontal and vertical axes independently so the tree always fills the screen with the newest generation at the bottom.
Split the population/plant chart into two graphs after sharing one axis made both unreadable at real scale.
Several rounds of default tuning on start population, mutation rate, time scale, and plant regrowth rate.
