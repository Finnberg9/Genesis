A generative-genome artificial-life sandbox. No fitness function — creatures only eat, survive, and reproduce. Bodies are grown from a variable-length genome; mutation duplicates, adds, and deletes parts, so body plans evolve that were never specified. Watch for emergent carnivory, defensive spikes, predator–prey arms races, and rising (then plateauing) complexity.

What's real here: generative (variable-length, topology-mutating) genome; emergent selection with no fitness function; gene duplication; emergent niches (land/water/air) and predation.   The honest boundary: the part vocabulary (leg/fin/wing/jaw/eye/gut/spike/neck) is finite — evolution recombines parts but can't invent a new part-type; biomechanics are abstracted to stats, not rigid-body physics. True open-ended evolution remains an unsolved research problem — watch the complexity curve plateau, and that plateau is the open-endedness wall appearing in your own data.
